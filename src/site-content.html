Therapy approach

Therapy should make sense to you — not just your therapist.

Psychology terms can sound more complicated than they need to. Here’s a plain-language look at the approaches that may shape your sessions.

for thought patterns

Cognitive Behavioural Therapy

A structured approach that explores how thoughts, feelings and actions influence one another — useful for anxiety, low mood and unhelpful thinking habits.

for rigid beliefs

Rational Emotive Behaviour Therapy

Explores the beliefs and meanings we attach to events — helpful when rigid expectations, self-criticism or all-or-nothing thinking create distress.

for the present moment

Mindfulness-Based Approaches

Builds awareness of the present moment without judgment — supporting stress, emotional reactivity, and learning to hold difficult experiences gently.

for emotional depth

Emotion-Focused Therapy

Treats emotions as meaningful information rather than something to suppress — helping with relationship patterns, needs, and self-understanding.

for practical goals

Solution-Focused Therapy

A future-oriented approach built around goals, strengths and small practical steps — useful when a focused problem needs structure.

always, above all

A Human, Personalised Blend

No single method fits everyone. Your sessions are shaped around your story, your pace, and what genuinely helps you — reviewed together as you go.
